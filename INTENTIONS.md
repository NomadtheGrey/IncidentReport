# INCIDENT REPORT — Intentions

What this game is, what it's for, and the rulings that don't get re-debated.
Maintained as decisions land. Pins are Adam's and are read back verbatim.
House dimensions, model standards, and working state live in NOTES.md.

## The game in one breath

A first-person home-invasion game that mocks its genre. The horror is
real, but the homeowner is competent, the house is a real house, and
everything in it behaves like itself. Horror's artificial helplessness
is inverted: competence wins, and it wins fast.

It grew from three seeds of Adam's: a game that mocks its tropes; a
truly terrifying scary game; and a scary game whose solutions would
work in the real world — e.g., bash the knife stalker with a frying pan.

## The one law — the Frying Pan Test

Every solution must work in the real world. The skillet works: a clean
hit puts the stalker down; whack again if he gets up; a dispatched
stalker stays dispatched until police arrive. Tying him up with an
extension cord is legitimate play.

No escalation gimmicks. (The "stalker returns wearing a motorcycle
helmet" idea was killed as movie logic.) No artificial mechanisms —
what works, works for the reasons it would work in a real house.

## Shape of it

The *Bambi vs. Godzilla* ratio: ~5 minutes of pompous opening credits,
~30 seconds of play, ~5 minutes of joke credits, closing on a grade /
report card — the titular Incident Report.

- First person.
- The house: upstairs, main floor, basement, attached garage.
- Adam playtests on his phone (S22). Everything must be testable there,
  and his playtest is the acceptance — a layout that can't be walked
  in a real house, or doesn't feel right in first person, bounces no
  matter how accurate it looks on paper.

## Pinned rulings

- **Title: INCIDENT REPORT** (Adam, 2026-11-09 — "just vague enough…
  and specific enough"). Retired: CAST IRON, DAD vs. STALKER, BONK.,
  SEASONED, REASONABLE FORCE, WRONG HOUSE.
- **Doors:** every door lockable from the inside, standard household
  doorknob privacy locks (2026-11-10). Swinging door leaves are fine.
- **Stairs are switchbacks** (Adam, 2026-11-10): "interior stairs tend
  to go down to a landing, then the person turns 180 degrees and
  descends the other half of the stairwell." Each stair box is 2.0m
  total, the divider built inside that 2.0m (each run ~0.95m clear).
  Half-flights are 3m; the half-landing is 2×2 and butts the house
  wall. Solid separator wall, not a railing — a railing leaves "too
  much room for shenanigans."
- **Closet / headspace rule:** floor over a stair run only where
  headroom below is ample; over the high portion the well stays open
  ("Unused / Reserved For Head Space"). The ceiling over stairs is
  squared off flat, not sloped, so the space above becomes the closet
  (Adam, 2026-11-11).
- **The layout is grounded in a real, walked house** — Adam's kids'
  house is the reference because he can walk it mentally; it didn't
  have to be their house. The point is a believable house, not a
  replica. Whole-metre grid.

## Not pinned — seeds, not promises

Adam over-generates on purpose; nothing below is settled until he
pins it.

- ~12 defences by room (kitchen skillet / boiling pot / fire
  extinguisher; living-room ashtray, poker, lamp-as-club — the
  electrified broken-bulb lamp jab FAILS the test: "That's not a
  taser. Just swing it."; upstairs bat, clothes iron, lock a door and
  call 911; garage claw hammer, let the guard dog in, get to the car).
- The sensible aftermath: tie him with extension cords / belts / duct
  tape, guard him with the skillet, lights on, door unlocked for
  police.
- The "you're the defendant now" branches: extra whacks while down
  (dispatcher: "Sir. Please stop hitting him."), dragging him to the
  basement, the deep freezer, digging a hole out back (hours vs a
  police ETA of minutes; garage shovel hover-text: "No."). Farce,
  not gore.
- The cover-story branch: lie to police while he's shackled in the
  basement; running gag — the Report's Suspect line points back:
  "VILLAIN: See Homeowner (above)."
