# INCIDENT REPORT

A first-person competent-horror game.

Horror's dominant design is artificial helplessness: no weapons, run and
hide only. This game inverts it. The threat is a believable human stalker —
that is where the terror comes from — but the home-dweller is **competent**,
and competence wins. Fast.

## The one law: THE FRYING PAN TEST

Every solution must be something that would actually work in the real
world, and everything in the world behaves like itself.

- The cast iron skillet works. A clean hit puts the stalker down, and if
  he tries to get back up, you whack him again. A dispatched stalker
  stays dispatched until the police arrive.
- No escalation gimmicks. No motorcycle helmets, no superhuman
  recoveries, no sequel-bait invincibility. Movie logic is the enemy.
- Tying him up with extension cords is a real thing, and legitimate play.

## The shape

*Bambi vs. Godzilla*: five minutes of pompous credits, thirty seconds of
play, five more minutes of credits. Long horror presentation, a noise
downstairs, a kitchen, a skillet that is right there because it is a
kitchen — bonk — a 911 call, and a closing report card that gives the
game its title.

The writing is the game: the credits jokes, the dispatcher's deadpan,
the Incident Report itself.

## The house

First person. Upstairs, main floor, basement, attached garage — a whole
ordinary house, and an ordinary house is full of things that behave like
themselves. See `DESIGN.md` for the pinned rulings and the house layout.
